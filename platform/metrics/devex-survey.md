# Quarterly developer survey

Ten core questions, the same every quarter, plus up to three rotating ones. Target under five minutes. Anonymous; results are reported by team, stack and workforce type (employee or vendor) only when a group has at least five responses.

Scale for 1 to 8: 1 = strongly disagree, 5 = strongly agree.

| # | Question | Dimension |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | It is easy for me to ship a change to production. | Flow |
| 2 | I get feedback from builds and tests fast enough to stay focused. | Feedback loops |
| 3 | Code reviews on my pull requests arrive in reasonable time. | Feedback loops |
| 4 | I can find the documentation and the owner I need without asking around. | Cognitive load |
| 5 | The tools and processes I use rarely get in the way of the actual problem. | Cognitive load |
| 6 | I can set up a new service or environment without waiting for another team. | Self-service |
| 7 | I am confident that a deployment I make will not break production. | Quality |
| 8 | Overall, I am satisfied with the developer platform. | Satisfaction |
| 9 | About how many hours did you lose last week to waiting, broken tooling or unclear process? (number) | Friction |
| 10 | What is the one thing that would most improve your day-to-day work? (free text) | Priorities |

## Rotating questions (examples)

- AI: "The AI tools I am allowed to use save me time." / "I trust the output enough to use it with a normal review."
- Onboarding (asked to people with under 90 days): "How many days until your first merged change?"
- Golden path: "If you chose not to use a golden path, why?"

## Running it

1. Announce what was done with last quarter's answers before asking again.
2. Keep it open one week, one reminder.
3. Publish results and the top three actions within two weeks.
4. Code the free-text answers into themes; the top themes feed the platform backlog.
